01 — The Science

Why people think, learn, and act the way they do.

Five scientific domains — each one a lens for understanding human performance.

The scientific foundation.

Everything Mentimeter builds is grounded in decades of peer-reviewed research. Five domains of science explain what actually drives how people think, learn, decide, and change — and what leaders can do to improve each of them.

Individual Cognition

How one person thinks in the moment. Attention is limited, working memory is small, and passive listening leads to shallow processing. Understanding this changes how you design any session.

Learning Science

How knowledge moves from short-term to long-term memory. Retrieval, generation, spaced repetition — the science is clear on what works. Most organizations do none of it.

Social Psychology

How groups think — and why they often think poorly. Status, hierarchy, and early opinions distort contribution. The right structure changes everything about who speaks and what gets said.

Decision Science

How decisions become high-quality. Vague criteria, biased questions, and inconsistent judgment ("noise") undermine most group decisions. Structure and clarity are the antidote.

Behavioral Science

How behavior changes — and sticks. Understanding motivation, intention gaps, and habit formation explains why people don't do what they said they would, and what actually works instead.

This science didn't stay in the lab. It shaped a clear point of view on how great leaders should act. Here's what we believe.

04 — Active Participation

How questions activate thinking — and thinking activates everything else.

HOW TO CREATE ACTIVE PARTICIPATION

Performance doesn't start with information. It starts when someone is invited to think. A single question changes everything: attention sharpens, thinking activates, engagement follows. This is the moment performance begins — and great leaders know how to create it, consistently.

01

Ask

Set intent and frame the moment. A clear, well-crafted question activates attention before anything else.

02

Think

Protect individual thinking. Give people time to form their own view before the group influences them.

03

Share

Make thinking visible. When input is surfaced and shared, diverse perspectives emerge and group intelligence rises.

04

Act

Turn input into action. Ownership increases when people shaped the direction — and follow-through comes naturally.

Reset attention continuously

Attention fluctuates. Moments of interaction re-orient focus and bring people back into active processing — before they've drifted too far.

Protect individual thinking

Early group opinions anchor everyone else. Capturing individual input first — anonymously — preserves independent judgment and surfaces more honest perspectives.

Distribute participation

When only a few voices dominate, collective intelligence drops. Structure ensures everyone contributes — not just the confident or the senior.

Create psychological safety

People hold back when there is social risk. Anonymity and inclusive design make it genuinely safe to share what you actually think.

Make thinking visible

Thoughts stay hidden until they're surfaced. Showing everyone's input creates a shared object to react to, discuss, and decide from.

Build feedback loops

People need to know their input mattered. Immediate, visible results close the loop — and fuel continued engagement.
